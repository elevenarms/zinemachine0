Thirteen gates between a routed board and a shipped product

AI agents generate, check and package every artefact. A named human signs each release, and two people sign where a mistake would cost a respin, a mould, a compliance failure or a customs penalty. Each gate below lists its owner, RACI, the artefacts the agents prepare, a product-specific checklist, the evidence required, what an agent may approve on its own and what it may not, the sign-off mechanism, SLA, escalation and rollback, and the failures it exists to catch. The failures are taken from this project's own history. Operating principles

  1. Agents execute and humans approve. Agents run make_fab.sh, the routers, the FreeCAD scripts, stock and price pulls, report parsing and document drafting. They never hold a signing key, never place a PO above Tier 0, never accept a sample from a vendor, never sign an SDoC, a DoC or a commercial invoice, and never make a final tariff classification.
  2. Evidence is tied to hashes. Every approval names a git commit and the SHA-256 of each released file: the Gerber zip, BOM, CPL, STEP/STL and firmware image. The board routers are non-deterministic (A Freerouting; R KiCadRoutingTools), so the release is the file, not the script that made it.
  3. Approval is measured against a baseline, not as an absolute. Results are compared against the last signed baseline. v2 carried around 106 inherited net_conflict parity items, so a bare DRC count meant nothing (A).
  4. The two-person rule. Two approvers. At least one did not write the change, and neither is an agent. It is required for every change that touches copper, steel, money above Tier 1, a legal declaration or the signed firmware.
  5. A missed SLA never counts as approval. A late gate escalates. It does not default to "go". Safety and compliance items cannot be auto-approved under any condition.
  6. Auto-approval only narrows. An agent may approve a re-release only when it is identical to, or a strict subset of, a change a human has already approved: same netlist, same parameters, same HTS line. It may never approve a first release.

Gate readiness

Readiness is the share of checklist items ticked for the selected strategy. Items that apply only to other strategies are left out of the count. Sign-off fields record who approved, and against which tag or e-signature envelope. Everything is stored in this browser's localStorage (zm3.gates.v1) and nothing is uploaded. A gate shows ready for sign-off when every item is ticked and every upstream gate is signed.

RACI matrix

R responsible (does the work) · A accountable (signs, exactly one per gate) · C consulted before sign-off · I informed after. The AI agents are R for preparing artefacts on every gate and never A. Click a row to open that gate.

Spend and PO release thresholds (G6)

Amounts are per PO, in USD, including freight and expected duty. Tiers apply to the committed amount, so a tooling deposit counts at the full tool value. Splitting a purchase to get under a tier is blocked: the agent sums all POs to the same vendor for the same budget line within 14 days.

Worked examples from the overview page (S): 5 JLC 4-layer proto boards with 2 assembled ≈ Tier 1. 100 × PocketBeagle 2 Industrial at $51.03 = $5,103 → Tier 2. Compliance NRE about $12.5k → Tier 2, but the lab is a new vendor, so the "first purchase" rule also applies. Aluminium lid, tray and chassis tools about $26k → Tier 3. The ~720-set tool payback point is a PO-level decision, not an agent's.

Where the gates differ by strategy

Domestic: US PCBA, moulding and box build. The PB2, panel and cells are still imported, so this is not "Made in USA" under the FTC's "all or virtually all" standard. USMCA: moulding and box build in Mexico (maquila/IMMEX), PCBA in Mexico or the US, preferential duty only if the product rule of origin is met. Global: Shenzhen contract manufacturer, PCBA at JLC, a Chinese moulder, and finished goods imported. Hybrid: PCBA and mould build in China, with the moulds transferred to a US or Mexico moulder, and final box build, eMMC flash, functional test and pack-out in the US.
