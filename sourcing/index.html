What it takes to build one, a hundred, or five thousand

A 40% Choc keyboard with a PocketBeagle 2 Industrial, a 4.58″ 320×960 bar display, a battery and a translucent slide-on lid. This page covers costs at five quantity tiers, two or three vendors per part, ready-to-upload BOM files, enclosure and tooling routes, a timeline to a pilot run, and the risks. Every figure is tagged: V verified live on 2026-11-07 · A from the repo / AGENTS.md (2026-11-05) · S search snippet only · E estimate. No figure here is a quote.

Executive summary

Headline per-unit cost, landed and boxed, with tooling and compliance amortised: at 10 units, at 100 and at 1,000. Without the one-off costs (NRE) the figures are , and . At 1 and 10 units the price assumes you build them yourself, so no labour is charged and nothing is shipped to customers. That is why 100 units can cost more per unit than 10.

The PocketBeagle 2 Industrial ($51.03 at DigiKey, verified live today, no quantity breaks) is the largest single part cost at every tier. (At 1 unit, JLC's one-off PCBA fees of ~$82 are larger still.) At 1,000 units it is about 30% of the cost. The other big items are the display, the main board, and switches plus keycaps. Below 100 units, fixed fees dominate: JLC PCBA setup and per-part loading, plus the minimum order of 5 boards. At 1,000, the tooling ($26k aluminium, estimate) and the compliance testing (~$12.5k) are what you amortise.

Blockers before ordering anything
  1. No display software exists. The DSS overlay and ST7701S init are unwritten, and the panel pinout comes from a different vendor's datasheet. Proto 0 exists to remove this risk.
  2. GCT USB1046 USB-A: only 74 at LCSC (2 per board, verified live today). 100 builds are not possible without a second source.
  3. The battery cell is not chosen, and no off-the-shelf cell fits 9 × 40 × 70 mm. Volume needs a custom pack with UN38.3.
  4. The repo's bom/ and pcb/…/fab/ files describe the v2 board (32U4, hot-swap). This page uses the v3 netlist from tools/zm_v3/design.py. The board's fab outputs are still listed as open in the README.
  5. The case was checked only against the PB2 EVT STEP. v3 uses the Industrial variant on 3.55 mm sockets: check it against case/pocketbeagle2/models/industrial/pocketbeagle2 revA_dimension.pdf.

Where the money goes

Price breaks, 1 → 10

The PCBA fixed fees (~$90) and the 5-board PCB minimum stop being carried by one unit: about −$120. Freight per unit falls by 80%.

10 → 100

Moving to Standard PCBA (panel limit above 30 boards) changes little. The real cost at this tier is compliance, if the units are sold: ~$125 a unit. Printed parts barely get cheaper; the 3D-printed lid stays at ~$30.

100 → 1,000

Moulded tray, chassis and lid fall from ~$43 to ~$7 per set. Custom battery: −$7. Direct PB2 and panel pricing (estimate): −$7. Bulk switches and custom-printed caps: −$9. Tooling adds back ~$26.

3 · Prebuilt carts (BOM uploads)

Each button builds a CSV in your browser. Nothing is uploaded anywhere. Uploading the file is the way in for every vendor: none of the add-to-cart URL formats could be verified.

  • DigiKey's classic/ordering/fastadd.aspx?part1=…&qty1=… appears in third-party code (May 2026), but a test without a browser session showed an empty cart. Its myLists API (/mylists/api/thirdparty, used by DigiKey's own KiCad plugin) needs a POST, so it can't be a link.
  • Mouser's cart API needs an API key.
  • LCSC has no URL-based cart.
  • Kitspace 1-click BOM is archived.
Quantities = per-unit qty × units, packs rounded up. The JLC files are per design and don't depend on the unit count.

JLCPCB PCBA (main board)

Columns Comment, Designator, Footprint, LCSC Part # / Designator, Mid X, Mid Y, Layer, Rotation, as in JLC's help articles. The CPL is generated from the v3 board file: KiCad origin, y negated (the same convention as kicad-cli pcb export pos). Check every rotation in JLC's placement preview; not done for v3. Switches, the PB2 and headers are excluded and hand-fitted.

LCSC BOM Tool (hand-assembly kit)

LCSC BOM Tool: CSV under 4 MB, up to 800 lines. You map the columns on upload, and Quantity plus one part-number column are required (verified live). Includes every board part and the PB2 headers. Use it to stock a hand-assembly kit or to consign parts JLC lacks.

Upload at lcsc.com/bom.

DigiKey (off-board parts)

PB2 Industrial, panel (Adafruit 5805; 8 in stock, 18 wk), proto LiPo, bumpons, panel tape, spare F32R. DigiKey part numbers are left blank so the upload matches on Manufacturer Part Number; you map columns on upload. No official header list was found.

Upload in myLists → Import or BOM Manager.

Mouser (off-board parts)

Mouser BOM Tool: XLS/XLSX/CSV under 4 MB, 99 lines by default; map Mouser or Manufacturer Part Number (snippet, help page timed out). The same parts as the DigiKey file, minus the panel tape. Mouser part numbers are blank.

Upload at mouser.com/bom.

Not in any cart

BuyDisplay panel (order on the site, and choose "No Touch Panel"). Switches and CFX keycaps (Chosfox, splitkb). M2 thread-forming screws (an M2 kit or McMaster). Printed parts (JLC3DP upload). Each of these sells through its own storefront.

Tool ranges: Protolabs aluminium from $1,500 and steel from $50k V; TEAM Rapid $2,500 rapid tool + $2.20/part at 1k and $12k steel + $1.20/part at 10k V. A slide adds 15–25% and a lifter 20–35%, with $4–10k per slide S; SPI A-1/A-2 polish costs $800–1,500 / $600–1,000 per cavity S. The per-part ranges in the table combine these and are estimates. JLC does not offer injection moulding (no service found under JLCPCB, JLCCNC or JLCMC), so use HLH, Firstmold, Star Rapid or a Shenzhen moulder found through your contract manufacturer.


  
When tooling pays off. Per set, printing costs ~$43 (SLA lid + MJF tray and chassis at 100) and moulding ~$7 (1k piece price). The ~$36 saving recovers ~$26k of aluminium tooling at ~720 sets, or ~$50k of steel at ~1,400 (estimate). With a 100-unit pilot and no clear path to 1,000, keep printing and urethane casting. Commit to aluminium tools once pre-orders pass ~700. Steel only makes sense for 5k+ or multi-cavity work.


  
DFM notes

  

    
Tray (opaque ABS or PC/ABS)
      
    
        
  • Draft: 1–2° on the outer walls; 1° per inch of depth; 3° at shutoffs; 3° for light texture (PM-T1), 5°+ for medium (PM-T2) V (Protolabs). The 12.5 mm walls need ~0.5–1°.
    • 
        
  • Side grooves are external undercuts along the full 131 mm length: two side-action slides (one per side), or move the parting line to the groove centre z 5.5 so the rails form in the two halves. The second option removes both slides, about $8–20k (estimate).
    • 
        
  • Thickened side walls (SIDE_ADD 1.5 → ~3.5 mm locally) next to 2 mm walls will sink. Core them out to a uniform 2–2.2 mm.
    • 
        
  • Boss OD 4.4 mm for M2 thread-forming screws: wall ≤ 60% of nominal, with a coring pin. Gussets help.
    • 
        
  • Gate on the underside, centred, behind the keyboard area (hidden). One gate is enough for 175 mm in ABS at 2 mm.
    • 
      

    
Lid (clear PC, PMMA or tinted MABS)
      
    
        
  • Snap detent nubs sit on the inside of the side walls, which is an internal undercut. Protolabs: bump-offs work in TPE/PE/PP; "polycarbonate… not likely to work very well" V. So PC needs two lifters (+20–35% each, S). A cheaper alternative: put a window in the wall behind each nub so a straight-pull core forms it, or switch to MABS and test a bump-off with a 30–45° ramp on the nub.
    • 
        
  • The rails are internal features along the full length: these also need lifters or collapsing cores, unless they are moved to the parting plane. A rail/detent DFM review with the moulder is the single biggest tooling cost driver.
    • 
        
  • Clarity: SPI A-1/A-2 diamond polish on the cavity (the visible outside) and A-2/A-3 on the core. Draft ≥1° on polished faces, 1.5–2° preferred, or ejection scuffs the polish.
    • 
        
  • Gate on a hidden edge: the open rear end of the lid, a tab or fan gate, so flow runs the long axis without jetting. Avoid pin-point gates on the top face. Expect knit lines around the display-bump step; vent there.
    • 
        
  • Wall uniformity: 1.6 mm everywhere. Tie the 1.0 mm edge radii into the walls with no thick junctions at the step (clear parts show every sink and flow mark).
    • 
        
  • PC: dry at 120 °C for 4–6 h to <0.02% moisture; mould at 80–100 °C. Watch stress cracking around the snap fingers and from cleaning solvents V. Specify UV-stabilised grades (SLA prototypes yellow).
    • 
        
  • 15 colourways = 15 tinted batches. Production should offer 3–5 lid colours with one or two neutral trays. Each colour change on a run costs purge time and scrap.
    •

Compliance and certification

  

    

      
The PocketBeagle 2 has no radio (no Wi-Fi or BLE in the official spec V), so the Zine Machine is an unintentional radiator under FCC Part 15 Subpart B. It needs a Supplier's Declaration of Conformity: no FCC ID, no TCB, an unaccredited lab is allowed, a US-based responsible party (the importer counts), a compliance statement and a model-number label V.

      
What the module does and doesn't cover: the PB2 is a board, not a certified module, and BeagleBoard publishes no FCC/CE declaration for it (the support page returned 404). Nothing carries over: the finished product is tested as a whole, with its 1.4 GHz SoC, DDR4, the 320×960 RGB bus to the panel (a typical emissions source), the USB hub and the boost converters. Even a certified radio module's grant only covers its transmitter; the host still owes Part 15B.

    

    

      
EU / UK: CE under the EMC Directive (EN 55032 emissions + EN 55035 immunity), safety under the GPSR (Reg. 2023/988) with EN IEC 62368-1 as the basis (the LVD does not apply: this is a 5 V USB device, below its 75 V DC floor; see Expert adds), RoHS (documented by supplier declarations), and WEEE producer registration in each country you sell to. UKCA is optional: GB accepts CE V. EU Battery Regulation Art. 11: from 18 Feb 2027 portable batteries must be user-removable and replaceable (with exemptions under discussion S). The battery behind four screws and a connector probably qualifies, but get that confirmed. Battery: the cell or pack needs IEC 62133-2 and UN38.3 (UN38.3 $0.3–1.7k S). Buy packs that already have both reports.

      
Budget: FCC 15B $1.9–5k V; CE EMC €0.8–2k emissions plus immunity, up to ~$8k S; 62368-1 safety assessment ~$2–4k E. Model total: ~$12.5k E. The USB-IF logo is optional ($3.5k admin + testing S), so skip it and don't use the logo.
