Verdict

The most-likely finish is consistently optimistic compared with the Monte Carlo P50. Carrier legs move on business days, so a one-day slip that pushes a delivery past Friday costs three calendar days, not one. That asymmetry is why P50 and P90 are the numbers to plan with.

Computing…

Yes, but only with the hybrid PCB plan, and only if the units are hand-carried or built where the user is. At the default inputs, hybrid with next-flight-out delivery gives P50 ≈ 9.5 days, P90 ≈ 10.6 days, and about a 3-in-4 chance of day 10. China quick-turn alone gives P50 ≈ 12.4 days (~20% by day 10). Domestic quick-turn gives P50 ≈ 15 days (0%): the ES8388, FE1.1s and IP5306 have no US authorised stock V, so a US assembler waits for an LCSC kit and then adds its own turn and two courier legs. Any plan that ends with a FedEx/UPS overnight hits 0%: sign-off falls on a weekend, the courier picks up on Monday and delivers on Tuesday (day 10.1).

The model leaves out resource contention beyond the two shared printers, and it treats delays as independent, though an 11.11 backlog would slow LCSC and JLC's DHL leg together. Treat the 3-in-4 as an upper bound; a fair planning number is 55–65%. The hybrid plan works because two independent routes race to "boards in hand": JLC turnkey PCBA by DHL, and US 1-day bare boards (Advanced Circuits) reflowed in-house from an LCSC kit. Each lands on about day 5–6, and whichever is first wins.

Conditions for under 10 days:

  1. Orders go in today (Saturday): JLC by Saturday evening, the bare boards in time for Advanced Circuits' Monday 12:00 MST cutoff S, and DigiKey / Adafruit / LCSC within 2–3 hours of starting.
  2. DigiKey's 8 Adafruit 5805 panels are still in stock when you order V. Its pin table matches J3 pin for pin (checked today against the HD458002C40 spec sheet V), so the BuyDisplay panel from Hong Kong is off the critical path.
  3. First light on a bench PocketBeagle within about 2.5 working days of the parts arriving on Tuesday. Firmware has that much float before the boards land. It is the critical path in only ~8% of runs at the default durations, but in 36% if first light takes 3+ working days. There is no published PB2 DPI overlay (forum question unanswered S).
  4. Three people working through both weekends: firmware; mechanics + fixture; reflow + build. A 0.4 mm-pitch QFN reflow needs someone who has done it before.
  5. Board #1 needs at most about one day of bodging, and validation stays a 2-hour soak plus one 1 m drop on a sacrificial fourth unit.
  6. Delivery by hand or next-flight-out (or the user is the builder), not an overnight courier.
What usually pushes it past day 10
  • Day 0 is a Saturday, and so is day 7. US vendors, Xometry and DigiKey don't ship until Monday, so the PB2, panels and test gear arrive on Tuesday (day 3). Couriers don't pick up on the second weekend either. Wednesday 11 Nov is Veterans Day and also China's 11.11 shopping peak, which loads LCSC and DHL.
  • DHL from Shenzhen. JLC ships DDP; total duty is about 35% for Section 301 goods V. No Sunday delivery.
  • First light on the panel: pixel-clock polarity, the panel's 80 px of hidden overscan (its RAM is 400 px wide) and 9-bit SPI on McSPI are each worth a lost day.
  • Keycaps: CFX caps only ship from China. Adafruit's Kailh caps are 17.5 mm wide S and collide at the board's 17 mm pitch, so the fallback is printing caps in-house.
Scope of "validated": every unit passes the EOL script (power, display, 43 keys, USB host and device, audio, battery) and the 2 h soak. One extra unit takes the drop test. All of them are checked against CAD and CTQ dimensions and signed off on a per-serial sheet. This is engineering validation of 3 prototypes, not DVT.

Why "domestic" doesn't mean faster here. tools/zm_v3/design.py picked LCSC parts. Three of them, the ES8388 (C365736), FE1.1s (C9359) and IP5306 (C181692), are not stocked by DigiKey, Mouser, Arrow or Newark; FindChips today lists only Win Source and brokers V. Any US assembler therefore waits for an LCSC DHL shipment (3–5 business days, estimated), which is about as long as JLC takes to build and ship the finished boards. Swapping in US-stocked parts would need a respin, which doesn't fit in 10 days.

Differences between the 5805 and what v3 was designed for

  • Outline 117.90 × 40.37 mm V, against the 118.8 × 41.57 mm the display chassis pocket was drawn for A. The panel sits 0.9 mm short and 1.2 mm narrow, so centre it with tape or a printed shim. The 110.30 × 36.77 mm active area matches the site model V.
  • Backlight: Vf 12 V typ., IL 40 mA typ. and 40 mA absolute maximum; LED life is rated at 20 mA V. The board's R1 = 5.1 Ω sets 0.2 V / 5.1 Ω ≈ 39 mA A, which is the absolute maximum. For the proto, cap the PWM duty at 50% in the device tree: the TPS61165 turns the CTRL duty into the FB reference, which should give about 20 mA E. Or fit R1 = 10 Ω.
  • RAM is 400 × 960 with 80 px of left overscan, according to Adafruit V. In DE mode the first 80 pixels of each line are hidden. See the bring-up steps.
  • The vendor init sets 3A 66 (18 bit/pixel) and C3 02 (DE mode; the file's own comment gives 82 for HV mode) V. The board grounds DB0 and DB12 and drives RGB565 on the other 16 lines A, so 18-bit mode is correct.
Day-0 shortcut: the Adafruit Qualia ESP32-S3 (5800, $19.95, in stock V) lists the 5805 as a known-working display and ships its init code and timings. Lighting the panel on a Qualia first proves the panel, the FPC orientation and the init sequence in about an hour, before any Linux work. If the PocketBeagle then shows nothing, the fault is on the AM62 side.

6 · Firmware bring-up: PocketBeagle 2 + panel on a breakout, before the PCB

This work can start today with no hardware (writing the overlay and the init script) and continue on a bench rig on day 3, when the PocketBeagle, the panels and a 40-pin breakout arrive. The bench rig is wired exactly like the main board, so the same overlay works on the PCB. Pad offsets below come from the repo's case/pocketbeagle2/models/industrial/pb2_header_pinout.csv A.

  1. Bench rig (day 3): PB2 Industrial, a 5805 on a Newhaven NHD-FFC40 40-pin 0.5 mm breakout (Adafruit's 1436 is out of stock V), and 22 short jumpers (≤10 cm, with a ground wire beside PCLK) following the J3 net map in design.py. Leave DB0 and DB12 on GND. Use a bench supply in constant-current mode at ≤20 mA, ≥13 V compliance for LEDA/LEDK. VCC is 3.3 V from P1.14.
  2. DSS overlay: tidss supports MEDIA_BUS_FMT_RGB565_1X16 in mainline V. Use a panel-dpi node with data-mapping = "rgb565", connected to the DPI video port. On AM62x the SK board's DPI is on &dss_ports port@1 E. Leave I2C1 unmuxed: VOUT0_D10/D11 share P1.36/P1.33 A.
  3. Timings: start with Adafruit's known-good numbers: PCLK 16 MHz, H 10 sync / 50 front / 30 back, V 2 / 17 / 14, syncs idle high (active low), DE active high, pclk_active_high = False V. With 400 active pixels that is 490 × 993 total, or 32.9 Hz. 60 Hz needs ≈ 29.2 MHz (computed); step up 16 → 24 → 29 MHz and watch for speckle on the jumper rig.
  4. Overscan: for first light, set hactive = 400 and accept 80 hidden columns. Then try HV mode (C3 82) with hactive = 320 and hback-porch = 30 + 80, so the hidden columns fall in the porch instead of the framebuffer E.
  5. SPI init: the mainline panel-sitronix-st7701 has an SPI (MIPI-DBI, 3-wire) path, but only for anbernic,rg28xx-panel V. For the sprint, don't write a kernel driver: send the vendor's 42-command sequence from userspace over spidev at 9 bits per word (D/C bit first) before starting the display manager. McSPI's word length goes up to 32 bits E; if 9-bit spidev fails, bit-bang the ~200 bytes with libgpiod. A proper st7701_panel_desc modelled on rg28xx_desc is follow-up work, and the Gantt shows it off the critical path.
  6. Backlight: P1.06 = EHRPWM0_A (pad 0x1AC, mux mode 6) A → pwm-backlight with levels capped at 50% (see the display notes above). On the bench rig the bench supply stands in for the backlight; PWM is tested on the first real board.
  7. Exit criteria: colour bars, then a walking-ones pattern on each of the 16 data lines with no stuck or swapped bits, a stable picture for 30 min, and a console at 320 × 960 (rotated to 960 × 320 for the bar).

7 · Key scan: "QMK on the 74HC165 matrix"

  

    

      
QMK doesn't apply to v3. v3 has no QMK-capable MCU (the ATmega32U4 went with v2). The 43 keys are wired straight to six chained 74HC165s on the PocketBeagle's MCU-domain pins: CLK P1.26 = MCU_GPIO0_13, SH/LD P1.28 = MCU_GPIO0_14, QH P2.05 = MCU_GPIO0_5 A. The design intent is M4F firmware plus RPMsg to uinput, and nothing for it exists yet A. For the sprint, use a no-code kernel path:

      
    
        
  1. spi-gpio (bit-banged SPI) on CLK and QH, with SH/LD as the load line;
    2. 
        
  2. pisosr-gpio, mainline's parallel-in/serial-out driver for exactly this '165 chain V, with ngpios = <48> and load-gpios;
    3. 
        
  3. gpio-keys-polled at a 5 ms poll over the 43 used bits, giving one evdev input device with full rollover;
    4. 
        
  4. layers and the three mode keys in keyd, taken from the Vial layout in the repo.
    5. 
      

      
Risk: mcu_gpio0 may be reserved for the M4F in the PB2 device tree. Fallback: a ~150-line C scanner with libgpiod plus uinput. Chain order: U10 bit 0 = K00 … U15 = K09/K19/K29/K39/RED/YEL/BLU A.

11 · EOL test script outline (Python 3 on the PocketBeagle)

  
Runs on the unit itself, booted from the golden image, and writes /var/log/zm-eol/<serial>.json. An operator follows the prompts on the bar display and on the fixture laptop over SSH. Paths are the expected sysfs names; confirm them on unit 001.

  
  






  
12 · Test fixture: pogo pins or USB?

  

    

      
Recommendation: a USB-based functional fixture now; pogo pins on the next revision

      
A pogo-pin bed needs test pads that the v3 board doesn't have: there is only TP1 (M4F spare) A. Adding pads means a respin. A bed-of-nails (laser-cut plates, P75 pogo pins, alignment pins in the M2 holes) also takes 3–5 days to design and build E. For 4 units, test the assembled device through its own connectors:

      
    
        
  • Printed nest (Bambu, 3–5 h) that holds the tray, with a cut-out over the slide switch and access to all rear ports.
    • 
        
  • All-keys press plate: a printed plate with 43 bumps, lowered by hand onto the caps, presses every key at once to test full rollover and the whole '165 chain in one step.
    • 
        
  • USB-C inline power meter on the charge port (logs V, A and Wh) and a bench supply as a battery simulator at 3.8 V, 3 A limit, on J5 for the current tests.
    • 
        
  • Two USB flash drives for the USB-A ports, plus a USB load (0.5 A per port) for the power-budget test.
    • 
        
  • TRRS loopback plug (tip→sleeve-mic through a 10 kΩ/1 kΩ divider) and a 3.5 mm TRS plug into the laptop's line-in.
    • 
        
  • Laptop on PB2 USB-C: USB gadget Ethernet for SSH and the device-mode test.
    • 
        
  • K-type thermocouples (battery, keyboard deck, PB2 area), a push-pull force gauge, calipers, feeler gauges.
