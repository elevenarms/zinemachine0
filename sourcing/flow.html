Tariff scenario (state as of 2026-11-07)

What changed in 2026 (verified 2026-11-07; tariff law is in active litigation, so re-check before acting):
  • IEEPA tariffs are gone. The Supreme Court held 6–3 in Learning Resources v. Trump (20 Feb 2026) that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs. CBP stopped collecting the "fentanyl" and "reciprocal" tariffs on 24 Feb 2026. Refunds are not automatic: they go to the importer of record via CBP's CAPE system under CIT supervision; a class was certified on 8 Oct 2026. V Zonos tracker (26 Oct 2026), Troutman (12 Oct 2026)
  • Section 122 (10% global, 24 Feb–24 Jul 2026) has expired. V STR
  • Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs apply to 60 economies from 24 Jul 2026: China +12.5%, Vietnam +12.5%, Malaysia +10%, Mexico/Canada +10% unless USMCA-qualifying. About 863 of 2,120 codes are exempt. Whether HTS 8471 is exempt was not verified: if it is, China's finished-device rate drops from 12.5% to 0%. Litigation was argued 30 Sep 2026, no decision yet. V Zonos, RVIA
  • The older China Section 301 lists still apply to parts: 8473.30 PCBAs on List 3 (+25%); non-EV Li-ion 8507.60 rose to 25% on 1 Jan 2026. 8471.30 portable computers (List 4B) were never collected. 178 product exclusions expire 9–10 Nov 2026. S/V
  • De minimis is suspended indefinitely (since 29 Aug 2025; CBP interim final rules 24 Jun 2026). Every shipment, including a JLC DHL box, makes an informal (<$2,500, MPF $2.77) or formal entry. S/V
  • USMCA: HTSUS General Note 11(p) deems 8471.30/.41/.49/.50 and 8473.30 goods originating when imported from a USMCA country, with no tariff-shift test (CBP ruling N359764, 31 Mar 2026; H312426). A Mexico-assembled device built from Chinese parts should qualify. Policy risk: on 1 Jul 2026 the US declined to extend USMCA, so it now faces annual reviews. V/S
  • Customs enforcement order EO 14411 (3 Jun 2026) tells CBP to tighten bonds and importer-of-record eligibility within 180 days. Expect bond and IOR cost changes. S

The counter-intuitive result: under these rules, importing parts costs more duty than importing the finished computer. From China, a PCBA pays about 37.5% (List 3 + forced labor) and a Li-ion pack about 40.9% (MFN + 301 + forced labor), while a finished 8471.30 device pays 12.5% (or 0% if exempt). That hits strategies A and D, which import parts and assemble in the US, and favours B (USMCA, 0%) and C.

How the model applies them: a layer adds its rate to the goods classes and origins it lists. Finished devices (class fg) pay on full transaction value at the border. In A and D the parts pay on entry (classes mod, disp, batt, keys, comp, hw, pcba), and US labour and enclosure are never dutiable. B pays 0% when the USMCA box is ticked. Duty is charged on value at the border (FOB transaction value under US rules, not CIF).

Export control (ECCN)

Likely 5A992.c, not EAR99 E, medium-high confidence. The device runs Linux with standard OpenSSL/TLS/SSH. That is encryption for data confidentiality, which takes it out of EAR99 and into Category 5 Part 2. It meets the mass-market criteria (Note 3 to Cat. 5 Pt. 2: sold to the public from stock, user cannot easily change the crypto, no substantial support needed), so it falls to 5A992.c. V BIS mass-market page

  • Since 29 Mar 2021, mass-market end items need no BIS submission and no annual self-classification report. The report under 740.17(b)(1) now covers mass-market components, executable software and toolkits only. V Thompson Hine 2021
  • 5A992.c ships "NLR" (no licence required) to most destinations. Embargoed and sanctioned destinations (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Russian-occupied regions; Russia/Belarus controls) and restricted end users still apply. Screen every international order against the Consolidated Screening List.
  • The ECCN only matters for exports from the US (international DTC orders, a Kickstarter shipping worldwide). It is irrelevant to importing.
  • Flag for export counsel: if the firmware ships non-standard crypto, or the M4F firmware adds anything beyond standard libraries, re-check. Keep the self-classification memo on file. The open-source exemption (EAR 734.3(b)(3)) covers the published source, not the hardware.

Method and limits

  • Bill of materials: the part prices (PB2, panel, cells, switches and caps, LCSC components, hardware) are the same in every strategy and come from the Overview page. Country of origin is China for all of them, so A–D are still mostly China-origin.
  • Process costs (PCBA, enclosure printing and moulding, tooling) by country are estimates scaled from the verified JLC fee table and the Protolabs and TEAM Rapid tooling figures. US, Mexican and Vietnamese EMS prices need real RFQs.
  • Freight: the shipment is costed per batch (units × 0.6 kg, 0.0016 m³ packed) with minimum charges, and short legs default to truck. Lead time adds component lead (PB2 98 d once a batch exceeds DigiKey's 3,442 units; panel 45 d at MOQ), the parallel PCBA/enclosure path, assembly, transit and customs.
  • CO₂e: tonne-km × a mode factor (well-to-wheel, GLEC-style, estimated; see the tariff/freight notes) plus a last-mile parcel allowance. It covers transport only: no materials, no manufacturing energy.
  • Not modelled: Mexican or Vietnamese import duties outside IMMEX/EPE zones, state sales tax, scrap and yield loss (add 2–5%), EMS margin above the estimate, demurrage and detention.
