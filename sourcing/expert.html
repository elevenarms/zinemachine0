● strong · ◐ partial · ○ none

Recommendation for v3: AOI on every board, X-ray on the ES8388 QFN and the IP5306 exposed pad for proto and pilot lots, then a sample. A pogo-pin functional EOL fixture on every unit. Flying probe on the first 10 boards of each new revision. ICT only if volume passes ~5k a year.

EOL test sequence (~90 s target; the limits are what go into SPC):

  1. Rails under a programmable load: +5V_SYS 5.00 ±0.25 V, 3V3 3.30 ±0.10 V, AVDD 3.30 ±0.05 V.
  2. Boot to a test image over USB, record PB2 serial/MAC and eMMC ID, write the device serial.
  3. Walk the 74HC165 chain: all 43 keys open, then a fixture actuator or operator press-sequence, 43/43 seen.
  4. USB hub: both USB-A ports enumerate a test stick; overcurrent flag trips at 0.5–1.0 A.
  5. Audio: loopback 1 kHz tone line-out → mic; THD+N and level within limits; jack-detect toggles.
  6. Panel: test pattern + camera or operator check; backlight current 39 mA ±10% (Cpk ≥ 1.33).
  7. Charger: charge current into a simulated cell or the real pack; charge-LED state; switch-off drops +5V_SYS.
  8. Flash the release image, log its SHA-256, print and apply the serial/QR label.

DFT asks for Rev B: test pads on +5V_SYS, 3V3, AVDD, KEY_CLK/SH/QH, the I2C3 bus, USB1 D±, and TP1 (M4F spare; it already exists). Keep pads ≥1.0 mm on a 2.54 mm grid where possible, all on one side.

CTQs and tolerance stack-up

Critical-to-quality dimensions come from mech/cover.py A. Choose a stack and a process, then edit any row. The tool gives worst-case and RSS (statistical, ±3σ) results against the requirement. Process tolerances: JLC 8001 SLA ±0.2 mm V; MJF ±0.3 mm E; injection-moulded ±0.1 mm E; PCB thickness ±10% E.

AQL sampling (ANSI/ASQ Z1.4, single sampling, normal inspection)

This uses the Z1.4 code-letter table (inspection levels S-1 to III) and the Table II-A acceptance-number pattern, reconstructed for this page. Suggested defaults: panels and cells at General II, AQL 0.65 major and 2.5 minor; moulded lid cosmetics at II, AQL 1.0 major and 4.0 minor.

Serialization and traceability (the digital thread)

Serial format: ZM3-YYWW-S-NNNNN-C. Product and revision, build week, site code (U = US, M = MX, C = CN, V = VN), sequence, and a mod-36 check character. It goes on a laser-etched or label QR inside the tray floor, plus a copy on the box. GS1 Digital Link is an option if retail needs it.

Captured per unit at EOL: PB2 serial + MAC + eMMC CID; main-board panel/lot + JLC order; panel lot; cell lot + UN38.3 report ID; lid/tray tool and cavity ID + resin lot; firmware image SHA-256; every EOL measurement with limits; fixture ID + operator; timestamps. Data goes to the PLM item record (see AI execution).

Uses: recall scoping by cell lot, RMA root cause, Cpk by fixture/site, CRA vulnerability scoping by firmware hash, warranty start date.

Cybersecurity and software lifecycle

  

    
EU Cyber Resilience Act
A Linux device sold in the EU is a "product with digital elements". Article 14 reporting (actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to ENISA/CSIRTs within 24 h, through the Single Reporting Platform) has applied since 11 Sep 2026, including to products already on the market. The essential requirements and conformity assessment apply from 11 Dec 2027. Fines run up to €15M or 2.5% of turnover. V law-firm alerts and the EC CRA page, 2026

    
What to build now
  • An SBOM (SPDX or CycloneDX) for every image: kernel, Debian packages, M4F firmware.
  • A vulnerability intake: security@ address, a security.txt, a CVD policy.
  • Signed OTA updates (e.g. RAUC or SWUpdate, A/B on the 64 GB eMMC) with a published support period.
  • No default passwords: per-unit credentials or first-boot setup.
  • AM62 secure boot exists on HS-FS parts: decide whether to use it (it fights hackability, so document the choice).

    
US
The FCC "Cyber Trust Mark" is voluntary. No US law equivalent to the CRA applies to this device. California SB-327 ("reasonable security", no default passwords) does apply to connected devices sold in CA. E from statute knowledge, not re-fetched today.

  






  
IP protection with overseas suppliers

  
    
    
  • The design is open (GPL-3.0 upstream). Protect what isn't: the brand, firmware signing keys, moulds and test fixtures, the supplier list and pricing.
    • 
    
  • Trademark: file in China (CNIPA, first-to-file) and the US before any factory sees the brand. About $1–2k through an agent E.
    • 
    
  • NNN agreement in Chinese, under PRC law, with a PRC court as the forum and liquidated damages. Signed before the RFQ goes out.
    • 
    
  • Split the work: China PCBA ships blank; images and keys are flashed at final assembly (strategy D/B) or at a controlled station with a hash log.
    • 
    
  • Tool ownership: title, mould marking, photos, location, insurance, release-on-request clause; payment milestones tied to T1 approval.
    • 
    
  • Watch for "ghost shifts" and clone listings: test-buy on AliExpress quarterly; the serial check-digit and cloud registration help detect clones.
    • 
  
Customs recordation: once the US trademark is registered, record it with CBP (e-Recordation, $190 per class E) so counterfeits can be stopped at the border.
Firmware: the GPL obliges you to publish source for GPL components. Keep proprietary bits (if any) in userspace, and make sure the M4F firmware licence is compatible.

Warranty reserve and RMA flow

  

  
RMA flow (lithium-aware)
  1. Self-serve triage: firmware re-flash and a FAQ handle most "dead" reports. Request logs and the serial number.
  2. If hardware is suspected, ask first for photos and the EOL record (via the serial).
  3. Advance-replace modules where possible (PB2, panel, lid) by mail, without the battery. Most repairs are user-swappable.
  4. Whole-unit return: prepaid label under UN3481 Section II. Swollen or damaged cells are forbidden by air: ground only, special packaging, or dispose locally.
  5. Inspect, root-cause, update the FRACAS, refurbish or recycle (WEEE / Call2Recycle).






  
Demand and forecast hedge

  

  
Staged commitments (the expert's real hedge)
  • Commit early to long-lead, single-source items at the P80 of demand (PB2: 14 wk; panels: MOQ), because running out costs far more than holding stock.
  • Commit to cheap, short-lead items (passives, caps, packaging) at P50; reorder in weeks.
  • Use pre-orders to turn demand into data and cash (see the cash curve on Material flow).
  • Negotiate flex windows (±20% within 4 weeks) and cancellation terms with the contract manufacturer.
  • Delay differentiation: build trays and electronics generic; choose the lid colour last (15 colourways).






  
Carbon accounting method

  
Method: GHG Protocol Product Life Cycle Standard / ISO 14067 for the product carbon footprint (cradle-to-grave), ISO 14083 / GLEC v3.1 for transport, and GHG Protocol Scope 3 categories 1 (purchased goods), 4 and 9 (transport), 11 (use) and 12 (end of life) at company level.

  
Data quality: start with secondary factors (estimates below); replace them with supplier-specific PCFs (BeagleBoard/Seeed, panel maker, cell maker) as they arrive. Report the uncertainty, not a single number.
